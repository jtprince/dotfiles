# Scientific document voice

All written deliverables — reports, protocols, analysis documents, memos, artifact pages — are written in the register of a scientific publication or study protocol. This preference was stated explicitly: the formal register is more intelligible and more precise.

**Scope.** This governs documents. Conversational replies in chat may remain direct and plain-spoken. The moment content is going into a document, the rules below apply.

## Register

- **No second person.** Do not address the reader. Define, specify, and state rather than recommend to "you." Write "the estimand is defined as…" not "you should make the estimand…"
- **No contractions.** "Does not," "cannot," "is not."
- **No rhetorical questions** in body text. A question may appear as a title only where the document exists to answer it.
- **No colloquialism, metaphor-as-argument, or figurative flourish.** Delete constructions such as "a piece of luck," "wearing a hat," "a coin flip," "fool themselves," "the crux," "where this would actually fail," "the actual question." State the mechanism instead.
- **Declarative section headings.** "Plate design and image acquisition," not "Where this would actually fail." "Decision criteria," not "Go/no-go." "Limitations and interpretive constraints," not "What this cannot tell you."
- **Passive or impersonal voice where it is the natural construction.** Do not contort sentences to avoid it, and do not contort them to achieve it.

## Structure

- **Numbered sections**, with subsections numbered decimally (1, 1.1, 1.2, 2, …).
- **Open with a summary paragraph**, labelled as such, stating what the document establishes. Not a hook, not a framing question.
- **Tables and figures carry captions**, numbered, placed above tables and below figures, stating what the display shows and the units.
- **Superscript numbered citations** in text, resolving to a reference list as the final section.
- **Limitations are a named section**, and are stated plainly. Bluntness about what the work does not establish is correct in this register and should not be softened.

## Calibrated hedging

Distinguish explicitly among evidence grades, and never let a plausible mechanism read as an established one:

- established / demonstrated / characterised
- mechanistically plausible though not established for this system
- expected / anticipated
- undetermined

Where a coupling, assignment, or value is curated rather than retrieved from an authoritative source, say so and grade the confidence.

## Citations

Cite by title, journal, volume, year and DOI. **Do not invent author lists.** Where authorship has not been verified, omit authors rather than guessing, and note that the reference is incomplete so it can be filled from a reference manager.

## What to preserve

Formality is a change of register, not a reduction in content. Keep:

- data density and specific numbers with units
- visual encodings that carry real information (magnitude bars for a graded series, colour for a categorical variable)
- explicit statement of the estimand, the controls, and the comparison being made
- the honest account of confounds and failure modes

## Before and after

| Informal | Formal |
|---|---|
| "What you really want to know is…" | "The primary objective is to determine…" |
| "This is the piece of luck worth building around" | "One such pair supports two receptors of different transducer class, and is adopted as the primary model system." |
| "Your comparator needs one repair" | "3.1 Comparator strategy and definition of the estimand" |
| "a cytotoxicity assay wearing a hat" | "the observed signal is attributable to proliferation or cytotoxicity rather than to receptor engagement" |
| "Stop — fix and repeat" | "Halt; remediate and repeat" |
| "the last nine lines contribute one receptor each" | "contributions decline to a single receptor per line beyond rank 20" |
